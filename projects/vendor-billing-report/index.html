Real scenarios this audit caught

Pulled directly from the flagged invoices — not illustrative.

1. The rate that quietly climbed 38% above contract

Hartwell Legal Advisors billed a May invoice at $427.48/hour against a contracted rate of $310/hour — a $7,965.14 overcharge on a single invoice, and it was sitting in "Pending Review" rather than caught before submission.

INV00217 | Hartwell Legal Advisors | 2025-05-25 Contracted rate: $310.00/hr Billed rate: $427.48/hr (+38%) 67.8 hours billed → $7,965.14 over expected
How it was caught: every invoice is compared against its vendor's contracted rate, not just checked for a plausible-looking total. A rate drift this size would sail through if the audit only sanity-checked total invoice amounts against a budget line.

2. The invoice billed for more hours than were logged

A different Hartwell invoice billed 90 hours at the correct contracted rate — but the note flags that billed quantity exceeded recorded usage, meaning the rate was right and the hours were wrong. This is the harder error to catch, because the total looks "reasonable" unless someone cross-checks against actual logged time.

INV00630 | Hartwell Legal Advisors | 2025-02-25 Rate: correct ($310/hr) Quantity billed: 90.0 hrs (exceeds recorded usage) Variance: $4,960.00 — and this one was already Approved
How it was caught: quantity is tracked as its own variance dimension separate from rate, so a "right rate, wrong hours" error doesn't hide behind a rate check that passes. This invoice had already been approved before the audit flagged it — a real gap between approval and verification.

3. The duplicate invoice that almost paid twice

ClearView Janitorial's June services were billed, then billed again days later under a separate invoice ID for the identical amount, vendor, and service period.

INV00018 | ClearView Janitorial | 2025-06-27 | $2,352.20 → a second invoice for the same vendor, date range, and amount was submitted separately and Approved
How it was caught: invoices are checked for matching vendor + amount + close date proximity, not just unique invoice IDs — duplicate invoice numbers are trivial to avoid by just incrementing a counter, so the check has to look at the underlying transaction, not the label on it.

4. The late fee nobody was told about

Vantage Software's November invoice came in $148.70 over the expected per-seat total — the rate and seat count were both correct, but a late fee got tacked on without prior notice, which is a contract-terms issue rather than a billing math error.

INV00528 | Vantage Software Inc | 2025-11-01 Expected: $7,140.00 (170 seats x $42.00) Billed: $7,288.70 Note: late fee applied without prior notice
How it was caught: variance flags are categorized by note/reason rather than left as an unexplained dollar gap — a $148.70 variance that's a disclosed-fee dispute gets routed differently than a $7,965 rate error, even though both are "variances."
