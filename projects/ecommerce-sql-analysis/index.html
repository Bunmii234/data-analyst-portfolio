E-Commerce Business Performance Analysis

Six business questions answered in standalone SQL against a relational e-commerce dataset (1,200 customers, 3,436 orders) — revenue trend, customer segmentation, cohort retention, channel performance, and category-level return rates.

Key findings

Revenue concentrates in Returning and VIP segments. They're only 52% of customers (627 of 1,200) but drive 79.5% of completed revenue. VIPs average $1,293 lifetime revenue vs. $301 for New customers — a 4.3x gap that supports investing in retention over pure acquisition.
Repeat purchase rate looks like it craters for recent cohorts — it doesn't. Mature cohorts (signed up 4+ months before the data cutoff) hold a 60-80% repeat rate, but cohorts from Feb 2026 onward show an artificial drop. That's right-censoring: those customers simply haven't had time to place a second order yet, not a real retention problem. Reporting this metric without excluding young cohorts would be misleading.
Best-sellers are also the most profitable. Every item in the top 10 by revenue sits at 55-69% margin — no "loss-leader" red flags, which is itself a useful negative finding for pricing strategy.
Paid Search and Email are the strongest acquisition channels by both total revenue (~$130K each) and revenue-per-customer (~$654-694) — ahead of Direct ($560/customer). Organic Search has fewer customers but a competitive AOV, worth not deprioritizing.
Return/cancellation rates are uniform across categories (11.0%-12.1%), suggesting a baseline cost-of-business rate rather than a category-specific quality problem.

The six queries

Query 1
Monthly Revenue Trend
Revenue and order volume by month, with month-over-month growth via a window function.
SELECT
    strftime('%Y-%m', order_date) AS order_month,
    COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) AS order_count,
    ROUND(SUM(order_total), 2) AS total_revenue,
    ROUND(
        100.0 * (SUM(order_total) - LAG(SUM(order_total)) OVER (ORDER BY order_month))
        / LAG(SUM(order_total)) OVER (ORDER BY order_month), 1
    ) AS mom_growth_pct
FROM orders
WHERE status = 'Completed'
GROUP BY order_month
ORDER BY order_month;
Query 3
Customer Segmentation (RFM-style)
Revenue contribution and spend per customer by segment (New/Returning/VIP).
SELECT
    c.segment,
    COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id) AS customers,
    ROUND(SUM(o.order_total), 2) AS total_revenue,
    ROUND(SUM(o.order_total) / COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id), 2) AS revenue_per_customer,
    ROUND(
        100.0 * SUM(o.order_total) / (SELECT SUM(order_total) FROM orders WHERE status = 'Completed'), 1
    ) AS pct_of_total_revenue
FROM customers c
JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id AND o.status = 'Completed'
GROUP BY c.segment
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;
Query 4
Repeat Purchase Rate by Cohort
Share of customers per signup-month cohort who go on to place a second order — the query behind the right-censoring finding above.
WITH cohort AS (
    SELECT customer_id, strftime('%Y-%m', signup_date) AS signup_month
    FROM customers
),
order_counts AS (
    SELECT customer_id, COUNT(*) AS completed_orders
    FROM orders WHERE status = 'Completed'
    GROUP BY customer_id
)
SELECT
    cohort.signup_month,
    COUNT(DISTINCT cohort.customer_id) AS cohort_size,
    ROUND(
        100.0 * COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN oc.completed_orders >= 2 THEN cohort.customer_id END)
        / COUNT(DISTINCT cohort.customer_id), 1
    ) AS repeat_purchase_rate_pct
FROM cohort
LEFT JOIN order_counts oc ON oc.customer_id = cohort.customer_id
GROUP BY cohort.signup_month
ORDER BY cohort.signup_month;
Query 5
Channel Performance
Revenue, AOV, and revenue-per-customer by acquisition channel — used to guide marketing spend allocation.
SELECT
    o.channel,
    COUNT(DISTINCT o.customer_id) AS customers,
    ROUND(SUM(o.order_total), 2) AS total_revenue,
    ROUND(AVG(o.order_total), 2) AS avg_order_value,
    ROUND(SUM(o.order_total) / COUNT(DISTINCT o.customer_id), 2) AS revenue_per_customer
FROM orders o
WHERE o.status = 'Completed'
GROUP BY o.channel
ORDER BY total_revenue DESC;

Dataset & method

Synthetic e-commerce dataset (SQLite, Jan 2024–Jun 2026): 1,200 customers, 3,436 orders, 8,605 order line items across 5 product categories. Built with realistic seasonality (holiday spike, summer bump), segment-driven purchase frequency, and category-level return noise — so the SQL surfaces real patterns rather than random data. Full reproduction steps are in the repo README.

Skills demonstrated: window functions (LAG), multi-table joins, CTEs, cohort analysis, and calling out a metric artifact (right-censoring) rather than reporting it at face value.
